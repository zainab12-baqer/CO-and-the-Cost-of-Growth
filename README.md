# CO-and-the-Cost-of-Growth
CO₂ and the Cost of Growth: Is Sustainability Still an Option?
Author: ZAINAB QAMBAR HUSAIN BAQER
Data Scope: 1980 – 2019
Case Study: Global, GCC, and Bahrain
### 📌 Project Overview
This project investigates the critical balance between economic expansion (GDP) and environmental preservation. By analyzing four decades of energy production and consumption data, the study explores whether energy-rich nations—specifically in the GCC—are leveraging their resources efficiently or at an unsustainable environmental cost.
### 🎯 Key Objectives
Trend Analysis: Tracking global energy consumption vs. CO₂ emissions (1980–2019).
Efficiency Assessment: Measuring how energy surplus correlates with economic growth.
Regional Comparison: Benchmarking Bahrain’s performance against GCC neighbors.
Actionable Insights: Providing data-driven recommendations for policymakers.
### 📊 Dataset & Methodology
The analysis utilizes a comprehensive dataset (Source: Kaggle) including:
Features: GDP (PPP), Population, CO₂ Emissions, Energy Production/Consumption.
Engineered Metrics:
Energy_balance: Production vs. Consumption.
CO2_per_capita: Environmental footprint per person.
Energy_Surplus_Class: Classification of energy-rich vs. energy-deficient nations.
### 🔍 Key Findings (Bahrain Focus)
Efficiency: Bahrain has shown steady improvement in energy efficiency over time.
Challenges: Energy intensity remains high compared to global benchmarks, and CO₂ per capita is a significant challenge.
Positioning: Bahrain holds a mid-range rank among GCC countries in terms of emissions per capita.
### 💡 Recommendations
Renewables: Transitioning to solar and wind energy to reduce carbon intensity.
Infrastructure: Enhancing industrial efficiency and power plant emission controls.
Monitoring: Implementing real-time tracking of energy intensity metrics.

Tools: Python (Pandas, NumPy for data handling).
Analysis: Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) & Feature Engineering.
Domain: Data Science / Environmental Economics.
